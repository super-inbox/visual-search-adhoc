# Brand Visual System — 荔泉气泡 (Lichuan Sparkling)

Every section below applies a rule from `PERSONAL_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §9 to this new brief. Full
rule-by-rule mapping is in `BRAND_SYSTEM_TRACEABILITY.md` — this document focuses on the resulting
design, not the justification (which lives in the traceability doc).

## Creative Direction

**One direction: "The Sprout-Bubble."** A single, original super-symbol — a bubble rising into a
sprout — carried identically across all three launch flavors, each flavor distinguished only by
its own dominant color field. Clean CGI can-render staging on a bright, condensation-lit ground.
No alternate directions are proposed; per the brief, this is the direction.

## Brand Idea

荔泉气泡 sells the moment a plant-based nutrient "wakes up" into something effervescent — the idea
that what's good for you doesn't have to be still, quiet, or dutiful. The central visual metaphor
is a bubble that is also a sprout: reinforcing "plant protein" (the sprout) and "sparkling" (the
rising bubble) as one single, simplified idea rather than two separate messages fighting for
attention on the can face.

## Super-Symbol

**Concept:** a single continuous outlined shape reading simultaneously as a rising bubble and a
germinating sprout — a rounded teardrop/bubble form with a small two-lobed notch at its top that
reads as a sprouting leaf-tip breaking the bubble's surface.

**Geometry:** one unbroken contour line, no internal detail — a rounded bubble base tapering to a
point, interrupted at the tip by a small V-shaped leaf notch. The whole shape sits inside a perfect
circle outline, echoing the portfolio's recurring circle-as-mark-container habit
(`PERSONAL_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §6 — circular container geometry observed in work_01, 05, 06, 09) —
the circle is a *container convention* borrowed from that recurring habit, not a copied logo; the
bubble-sprout shape inside it is original to this brand.

**Meaning:** the bubble reads as effervescence/sparkling; the sprout notch reads as plant-origin
nutrition — together they encode the brand's whole positioning ("plant protein" + "sparkling") in
one mark, the same way the source studio's marks were observed to encode product meaning rather
than serve as arbitrary decoration (`PERSONAL_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §5, "symbol-to-category meaning
relationship," evidenced in work_01, 05, 07, 09).

**Repetition across SKUs/assets:** the mark is reproduced at identical scale, line weight, and
position (upper-center front panel) on all three SKUs, in a single constant fill color regardless
of each can's own dominant background hue — directly mirroring work_05's identical "©CA" mark
repeated across three differently-colored SKU boxes (`PERSONAL_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §9 Rule 2). The
same mark appears again, at reduced scale, as a small repeating watermark motif in campaign and
social assets, never redrawn or re-styled per touchpoint.

## Color Behavior

Per Rule 1 (one dominant hue + one small accent, no multicolor clutter within a single SKU) and
the work_05 precedent for how a *family* of SKUs varies hue while a shared mark stays constant:

- **One shared brand accent, constant across all three SKUs**: a bright, slightly warm off-white
  ("spark white") used only for the super-symbol's fill and the pull-tab ring — this is the
  element that stays visually fixed no matter which flavor you're holding.
- **One dominant hue per SKU**, each a different, single saturated color family (never combined
  with a second dominant color on the same can):
  - Lychee Rose SKU: a soft warm blush/coral field.
  - Cucumber Mint SKU: a cool, clear mid-green field.
  - Yuzu Ginger SKU: a warm pale gold field.

These are **newly designed brand values for 荔泉气泡**, chosen to fit this brief — they are not
extracted, sampled, or estimated from any source-portfolio image, and no exact hex value is
specified here (per task instruction and per `PERSONAL_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`'s own refusal to claim
exact hex values from the evidence base).

## Typography Direction

Per Rule 3 (systematic bilingual Chinese + Latin presence, hierarchy decided per brief, not fixed):

- **Chinese leads**: "荔泉气泡" set as the largest, most prominent display text on the can,
  reflecting this brand's China-market-first identity — the same large-native-mark-leads pattern
  observed in work_01 (摘要), though rendered in a clean modern sans/rounded display style, not
  work_01's calligraphic/seal-script treatment, since that specific art style is not being copied.
  - Chinese role: the brand's primary spoken/remembered name; carries the most visual weight.
- **English is a smaller, supporting line**: "LICHUAN SPARKLING" set in a plain geometric sans,
  positioned directly beneath the Chinese mark at roughly a third of its scale — matching the
  large-mark/small-Latin-line hierarchy seen in work_01 and work_03, rather than the closer-to-parity
  bilingual pattern seen in work_09 (a real alternative in the evidence, deliberately not chosen
  here, since this brand's Chinese name is the more distinctive asset).
  - English role: legibility/export-shelf support and reassurance of "sparkling water" category
    clarity for non-Chinese-reading contexts.
- **Flavor name** ("荔枝玫瑰" Lychee Rose, "黄瓜薄荷" Cucumber Mint, "柚子姜片" Yuzu Ginger) sits
  below the English line, smaller again — a third typographic tier, consistent with the
  "utility/informational text stays small and dense" observation in `PERSONAL_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §3.
- **Tone**: clean, geometric, unfussy — no decorative or script typefaces, consistent with this
  brand's contemporary/everyday register (§2, §8) rather than the studio's heritage register. No
  specific source-portfolio typeface is claimed or referenced as an identity, per instruction and
  because none was reliably identifiable from the evidence.

## Composition & Layout

- **Hierarchy** (front can face, top to bottom): super-symbol (primary) → Chinese brand mark
  (secondary) → English line (tertiary) → flavor name + small botanical accent icon (quaternary) —
  directly following the "device first, color field second, wordmark and utility text last" ordering
  observed in `PERSONAL_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §4.
- **Whitespace**: moderate-to-generous — this brand sits in the "soft-goods/wellness" tone bucket
  (§2, §4), which the evidence associates with lighter density (work_02, 04), not the dense,
  prop-filled heritage staging of work_01/06. The can face itself stays clean; only the shared
  dominant-hue field, the symbol, and three type tiers occupy it — no secondary pattern or texture
  fills the remaining space.
- **Front-panel composition**: super-symbol centered in the upper third of the can face at a scale
  large enough to be legible from typical shelf viewing distance (deliberately the single largest
  graphic element on the can, per Rule 2's "large or central placement" observation) — not shrunk to
  a corner mark.
- **Symbol scale**: identical across all three SKUs (see Super-Symbol §, "Repetition").
- **Bilingual naming placement**: Chinese and English are stacked vertically, centered, directly
  beneath the symbol — never side-by-side, so the size contrast between the two languages (large
  Chinese, small English) reads clearly rather than being ambiguous in a horizontal layout.

## Imagery / Rendering

Per Rule 5 (CGI-rendered mockup is the default, real photography an occasional secondary mode) and
Rule 6 (staging is category-conditioned):

- **Lighting**: soft, bright, directional studio lighting with a single clear highlight — brighter
  and cooler than the single-source dramatic lighting used for the source portfolio's heritage/
  premium work (work_01, 03, 05, 06's dark-ground drama), consistent with this brand's placement in
  the "light/pastel, soft-goods" category bucket (§2, §4, Rule 6) rather than its heritage bucket.
- **Background**: a bright, near-white or very pale cool-toned ground with soft natural shadow —
  matching the light-backdrop branch of Rule 6 (work_02, 04, 08, 10), not the dark/black/maroon
  branch used for heritage-category work.
- **Product staging**: single-can hero shots show visible condensation droplets and a soft
  reflection, reinforcing "cold, sparkling, refreshing" without needing additional props — a
  simpler staging convention than the prop-dense heritage still-lifes in the source evidence,
  appropriate to this brand's cleaner, less ornamental register.
- **Realism**: photoreal CGI rendering (matching the studio's default per Rule 5), not flat vector
  illustration.
- **When lifestyle photography may be used**: real environmental/lifestyle photography (e.g. the
  can in-hand at a desk, or in a bag/commute context) is reserved for the social media post and
  campaign poster touchpoints only — mirroring Rule 5's finding that real photography is a valid
  *secondary* mode (work_02, 08), used deliberately rather than as the brand's default. The hero
  visual and the 3-SKU packaging family shot stay CGI-rendered.

## SKU / Family System

Three launch SKUs, presented as one ensemble/family (per Rule 7 — applies once a brand has 2+
SKUs, directly mirroring work_05's hero-plus-lineup and work_09's four-format lineup logic):

| SKU | Flavor name (CN / EN) | Dominant hue | Shared elements | Distinguishing elements |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 荔枝玫瑰 / Lychee Rose | Soft blush/coral | Sprout-bubble mark (spark-white fill, identical scale/position), Chinese/English wordmark stack, can silhouette, shared accent | Small lychee-blossom line-icon beside the flavor name (quaternary scale only) |
| 2 | 黄瓜薄荷 / Cucumber Mint | Cool mid-green | Same as above | Small mint-leaf line-icon beside the flavor name |
| 3 | 柚子姜片 / Yuzu Ginger | Warm pale gold | Same as above | Small yuzu-slice line-icon beside the flavor name |

**Consistency mechanism**: the super-symbol, its constant accent color, the can silhouette, and the
full typographic hierarchy never change between SKUs — only the dominant background hue and one
small quaternary-scale botanical icon change. This is the same mechanism observed in work_05 (one
fixed mark across three different SKU colors) and work_09 (one color/character system spanning
four formats), applied here as *hue-varies-mark-stays-constant* rather than *mark-varies*, so the
three cans read unmistakably as one family from across a room, while remaining distinguishable by
color up close.

## Packaging / Form

**Baseline**: a standard, off-the-shelf 330ml slim aluminum beverage can with a matte print wrap —
fully feasible with any standard beverage co-packer, no custom tooling required.

Per instruction, "form-as-symbol" (Rule 4) is treated as **optional and aspirational, not baseline**,
given its thinner evidence base (`PERSONAL_SYSTEM_VALIDATION.md` §2 — Rule 4 depends partly on the
partially-rendered work_10). One optional, low-risk application is proposed rather than a custom
silhouette: a subtle embossed ring texture around the pull-tab, echoing the super-symbol's circular
container — a standard beverage-can embossing technique, not a novel structural form. If production
cost or timeline doesn't support even this minor embossing, the system is fully complete without it
— no part of the brand identity depends on it.

## Do / Don't

**Do:**
- Keep the sprout-bubble mark identical in shape, line weight, scale, and fill color across all
  three SKUs and all campaign assets.
- Keep each SKU to one dominant hue plus the one shared spark-white accent — never introduce a
  second dominant color on any single can.
- Keep Chinese larger than English, stacked vertically beneath the symbol, on every asset.
- Default to clean CGI can renders with bright, soft lighting and visible condensation for hero and
  packaging-family assets.
- Reserve real lifestyle photography for social and poster touchpoints only.
- Present the three SKUs together as a family whenever more than one appears in a single asset.

**Don't:**
- Don't redraw, recolor, or restyle the super-symbol per SKU or per campaign — it must stay the one
  fixed anchor.
- Don't add a second dominant color, a busy pattern, or a heritage/ornamental illustration style to
  any can face — that would drift this brand toward the source portfolio's premium-spirits register
  (work_01, 06), which does not fit a plant-protein sparkling water brief.
- Don't invent or imply an exact hex value, a named typeface, or a specific print/material spec as
  if it came from the source portfolio — every color and type choice here is a new brand decision,
  not an extracted fact.
- Don't force a custom bottle/can silhouette to satisfy "form-as-symbol" — it is optional, and the
  standard can baseline is the complete, correct answer if a custom form isn't feasible.
- Don't let flavor photography or botanical icons grow larger than the super-symbol or the Chinese
  wordmark — they are quaternary-tier details, not the brand's primary visual anchor.
