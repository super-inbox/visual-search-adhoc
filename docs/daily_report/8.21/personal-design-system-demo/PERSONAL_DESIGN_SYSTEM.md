# Personal Design System — 造物起异包装设计

Extracted from `PORTFOLIO_EVIDENCE.jsonl` (10 works, 1 cover image each — see
`PORTFOLIO_SOURCE.md` for the full evidence base and its limitations). Every claim below cites the
`work_XX` IDs that support it. No exact hex values, font names, or physical measurements are
claimed anywhere in this document — only recurring tendencies visible across cover-image evidence.

---

## 1. Core Visual Character

Across the 10 sampled works, this studio's output reads as **confident, symbol-first commercial
packaging design**: each brand gets one clear dominant color identity and one deliberately
simplified graphic device, staged as a polished CGI product mockup rather than left as flat print
comps (work_01, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 09). The studio moves fluently between two registers —
ceremonial/heritage (work_01, 06) and playful/contemporary (work_05, 07, 09) — but keeps the same
underlying discipline in both: reduce the brand to one repeatable mark or move, then apply it
consistently across formats (work_05, 06, 09). It is not a single "look" so much as a consistent
*method* applied to different visual vocabularies per brief.

## 2. Color Behavior

- **One dominant hue family per brand**, not a multicolor palette: gold/maroon (work_01), sage
  green (work_02), near-monochrome black/grey (work_03), lavender (work_04), green (work_08, 10),
  periwinkle-blue (work_07), burgundy/orange (work_06), red/white (work_09). Seen in effectively
  all 10 works.
- **A single small saturated accent color** is then layered on top of the dominant hue, rather than
  introducing a second co-equal color: red ribbon/circle on gold (work_01), coral/blue tags on mint
  (work_02), one blue object on black/grey (work_03), gold cutlery/seal on burgundy (work_06), pink/
  green stickers on periwinkle (work_07).
- **Contrast pattern**: restrained monochrome-plus-accent (work_03) sits at one end; richly layered
  warm-on-warm (work_01, 06) sits at the other. Both still follow the "one dominant + one accent"
  logic — the studio varies *saturation and warmth*, not the underlying color structure.
- **Category-linked variation**: CPG/food/heritage work tends toward warm, saturated, or dark
  grounds (work_01, 06, 09); soft-goods/wellness/children's work tends toward pale, cool, or pastel
  grounds (work_02, 04, 07, 08, 10); brand-identity/stationery work goes near-monochrome (work_03).
  This looks like a deliberate register choice per brief, not an inconsistency.

## 3. Typography Tendencies

- **Bilingual Chinese + Latin naming recurs as a systematic feature** (work_01: 摘要/ZHAI YAO;
  work_03: 文创芝/artevive; work_04: Chinese size/care info alongside "Yuanqigirl"; work_09: 山楂
  粉/Hawthorn powder, 山楂条/Hawthorn strip) — but the *hierarchy* between the two languages is not
  fixed: work_01 and work_03 use a large native/display mark with a much smaller Latin line, while
  work_09 gives Chinese and English closer to equal visual weight per product name. Treat "bilingual
  pairing exists" as the stable observation and "which language dominates in size" as brief-specific,
  not universal.
- **Display type is used boldly when it's the hero device** (work_03's oversized "artevive"), but
  recedes to a supporting role when an illustrated motif or mascot is the hero (work_01's dragon,
  work_09's grandpa character) — wordmark prominence appears to trade off against motif prominence
  rather than both competing at full scale simultaneously.
- **Utility/informational text is kept small and dense** relative to the display mark or motif
  (work_01's Latin line, work_04's woven care tag, work_09's per-SKU bilingual labels) — no sampled
  work shows utility copy competing for primary visual weight.
- Exact typeface identities cannot be determined from screenshot-resolution cover images and are
  not claimed here.

## 4. Composition & Layout

- **Two staging modes, not one**: dramatic asymmetric or diagonal still-life on a dark ground
  (work_01, 03, 05, 06) versus clean, more centered arrangement on a light/pastel ground (work_02,
  04, 07, 08, 10). Within a given mode the studio is consistent — e.g. work_03 and work_05 both use
  a foreground hero object against black with supporting elements arranged behind/around it.
- **Ensemble/family compositions** are used specifically when a product line has multiple pieces or
  SKUs to show together: work_05 (SKU family behind a hero comb), work_06 (multi-piece gift set),
  work_09 (four package formats side by side). Single-SKU works (work_01, 02, 04, 07, 10) instead
  use a single hero object.
- **Whitespace density tracks category/tone**, not a fixed studio rule: low (dense, prop-filled)
  for heritage/ceremonial or hero-object shots (work_01, 05, 06, 07), moderate-to-high for soft
  pastel wellness/apparel work (work_02, 04). Cannot be assessed for work_10 (partial render) or
  work_08 (real environmental photo, not a controlled composition).
- **Front-panel hierarchy** consistently reads: primary graphic device/motif first, dominant color
  field second, wordmark and utility text last (clearest in work_01, 05, 09) — this ordering was
  not contradicted by any sampled work, though not every work has all three tiers visible in its
  single cover shot.

## 5. Super-Symbol / Graphic Device Methodology

This is the studio's own stated methodology (per work_09's title, "山楂爷爷超级符号视觉策略," and
this repo's prior research on the studio's stated process checklist — see `PORTFOLIO_SOURCE.md`).
The cover-image evidence supports the following concrete mechanics:

- **Simplification**: symbols are reduced to a single legible shape rather than a detailed scene —
  an abstracted eye/swoosh mark (work_05), a small circular floral roundel (work_06), a small
  circular leaf/arrow mark (work_09), an abstracted folded-ribbon/tick shape (work_03). Even the
  most ornate motif in the sample (work_01's dragon) is rendered as continuous flat linework, not
  rendered/shaded illustration — simplification is a consistent instinct even at high ornamental
  density.
- **Repetition as the memory-anchor mechanism**: the same fixed mark is reused verbatim across every
  SKU in a family regardless of that SKU's own color — work_05's circular "©CA" mark appears
  identically on lime, lavender, and grey boxes; work_06's floral roundel repeats on at least two
  separate tags in one gift set; work_09's circular mark and character motif tie together box, bag,
  tube, and card formats. This is the clearest, most literal evidence for "symbol repeats across
  package" in the sample (work_05, 06, 09 — 3 independent works).
- **Scale and placement behavior**: the symbol is generally given the largest or most central
  placement available in the frame relative to other graphic elements — foreground and
  large-scale in work_05, centered on the box face in work_09, wrapping the entire product surface
  in work_01. It is not used as a small corner mark in any sampled work.
- **Symbol-to-category meaning relationship**: where the connection is checkable, the symbol
  directly encodes the product/brand concept rather than being arbitrary — work_01's dragon motif
  and book-shaped bottle evoke heritage/classical culture for a premium spirit; work_07's cat-paw
  motif directly matches the product's own name (猫爪/cat paw); work_09's grandpa character
  personifies the brand name (山楂爷爷/Hawthorn Grandpa); work_05's comb-shaped product literally
  embodies its own logomark. Four independent works (01, 05, 07, 09) support "the symbol means
  something about the product," not just "the symbol looks nice."

## 6. Shapes / Motifs / Illustration

- **Repeated geometry**: the circle/roundel recurs as a logo-container shape across otherwise
  unrelated brands — work_01 (small red circle in the wordmark), work_05 (circular "©CA" mark),
  work_06 (circular floral roundel), work_09 (circular leaf/arrow mark). Four works — the single
  most consistent geometric habit found in the sample.
- **Mascot/character abstraction** appears in two clearly different registers: ornamental
  creature-linework in a heritage mode (work_01's dragon/phoenix) versus a named, personified folk
  character in a contemporary-snack mode (work_09's grandpa). Both are illustrative rather than
  photographic, but the *style* of illustration is brief-specific, not a fixed house style.
- **Borders/framing**: no sampled work shows a strong repeated border or frame device around
  content — this was looked for and not found; do not assume one.
- **Illustration behavior**: illustration is used either as full-surface continuous pattern
  (work_01's wrapping dragon line-art) or as a single discrete character/icon placed on one panel
  (work_07's cat paw, work_09's grandpa) — not as small scattered decorative sprinkles, except for
  the sticker-shaped callout tags noted below.
- **Sticker/blob-shaped secondary elements**: organic, rounded-edge "sticker" callout shapes recur
  in exactly 2 works (work_02's flower die-cuts, work_07's "Pure and natural" callouts) — enough to
  flag as a real but secondary device (see Signature Rule 8, medium confidence), not a headline
  trait.

## 7. Packaging / Form Language

- **Form-as-symbol**: in three works, the physical package/product silhouette itself is shaped to
  embody the brand concept rather than being a generic carrier for printed graphics — work_01's
  book-shaped bottle (echoing "摘要"/excerpt), work_05's comb whose handle shape echoes its own
  logomark, and work_10's tree-shaped bottle cap (weakest evidence — this work's image is only
  partially rendered; see caveats in Signature Rule 4 and `PERSONAL_SYSTEM_VALIDATION.md`).
- **Front-panel hierarchy adapts to physical format**: rigid boxes carry the fullest graphic
  treatment (work_01, 04, 06, 09), tubes/bottles carry a condensed version of the same mark/color
  system (work_09's tube, work_10's cap), and soft/flexible formats (work_02's folded product,
  work_09's pouch bag) carry a simplified subset rather than the full system.
- **Multi-format consistency**: where a brand spans several physical formats, the connective tissue
  is either one repeated small mark (work_05, 09) or one consistent color system (work_09), not a
  literal repeated layout — the studio adapts composition per format while keeping the identifying
  device constant.

## 8. Brand Personality

Only patterns visible in 2+ works are treated as personality signal, not single-work impressions:

- **Confident and legible over subtle or minimal** — every sampled work leads with one strong
  focal device (color field, symbol, or character) rather than restrained/minimalist negative
  space, even in the calmer pastel works (work_02, 04).
- **Culturally rooted when the brief calls for it, playful when it doesn't** — heritage/classical
  reference is deployed specifically for premium/gifting categories (work_01, 06), while lighter,
  friendlier illustration is deployed for everyday/FMCG/children's categories (work_07, 09) — the
  studio does not default to one national-aesthetic register across every brand.
- **Systems-minded rather than single-asset-minded** — the recurring instinct to repeat one mark
  across a whole product family (work_05, 06, 09) suggests the studio thinks in terms of a
  reusable system per brand, not a one-off hero image.

## 10. Do / Don't

**Do:**
- Pick one dominant hue family for the whole brand before anything else, then add exactly one
  small accent color.
- Design one simplified, repeatable mark and carry it, unchanged, across every SKU/format.
- Give the brand a bilingual (Chinese + Latin) naming presence somewhere in the system.
- Default to a clean, photoreal rendered mockup with intentional studio lighting.
- Match backdrop mood to category: dark/dramatic for heritage or premium, light/pastel for
  soft-goods or wellness/children's work.
- When there's more than one SKU, show them together as a family, anchored by the repeating mark.
- Consider shaping the package/product form itself around the brand concept when the brief and
  budget allow it.

**Don't:**
- Don't give a brand two or more co-equal dominant colors — keep the accent genuinely minor.
- Don't design a fresh, unrelated graphic for every SKU in a family — that breaks the
  memory-anchor mechanic that is this studio's core method.
- Don't assume a single fixed illustration style (ornamental linework vs. flat-abstract vs.
  mascot-character) applies to every brand — the style is chosen per brief, not house-wide.
- Don't assume Chinese always leads Latin in size, or vice versa — decide per brief.
- Don't rely on a border/frame device — none was observed in this sample.
- Don't invent exact colors, fonts, or measurements when generating — describe them the way this
  document does (hue family, weight/contrast behavior), not as fabricated specifics.

## 11. Uncertain Signals

as stable preferences:

- **Natural/kraft cardboard as a material choice for "softer-touch" categories** — only work_07.
  Could be a real preference or could be a one-off client requirement; not enough evidence either
  way.
- **Literal illustration of the brand's own name as a visual pun** (book bottle for "excerpt," cat
  paw for "cat paw" tissue, grandpa character for "Hawthorn Grandpa") — genuinely present in 3
  works (01, 07, 09) and numerically would qualify as HIGH, but it is a more interpretive/subjective
  read than the other rules (it requires inferring designer *intent* about the brand name, not just
  describing what's visible) — kept here rather than promoted to Section 9 so it isn't mistaken for
  as mechanical a rule as, say, Rule 2's mark repetition. Worth testing in Phase 3, not worth
  hard-coding as a requirement.
- **Diagonal composition/color-blocking as a layout device** — only work_03 (diagonal staging) and
  work_04 (diagonal color-blocked stripes); 2 works, technically meets MEDIUM threshold but the two

- **Whitespace/density as a fixed studio preference** — density tracked category/staging-mode in

## 12. Agent Generation Guide

When generating a new brand using this system, follow the **design logic**, not any specific
portfolio work:

1. Start from the brief's category and tone, and choose *which* of the studio's two registers
   applies: heritage/ceremonial (dark backdrop, warm/rich hue, ornamental or classical motif) or
   contemporary/everyday (light/pastel backdrop, softer hue, simplified or character-based motif).
   Do not default to one register regardless of brief — this studio conditions its whole visual
   approach on category (Rules 5, 6; Section 2, 4).
2. Choose one dominant hue family for the brand. Add exactly one small saturated accent color —
   resist the urge to add a second dominant color (Rule 1).
3. Design a single simplified graphic mark (the "super-symbol") before designing any individual
   package. Make sure it is simple enough to reproduce identically, at consistent scale and
   prominent placement, across every SKU or format the brand needs (Rule 2; Section 5). Choose its
   illustration style (ornamental line-art, flat abstract geometry, or a named character) based on
   the brief's tone — don't assume one style fits every brand.
4. If the brand has a name or concept with a clear physical or visual metaphor, consider expressing
   it in the package's actual form or in the mark itself, not only in surface graphics — this
   studio does this when the brief allows it, though it is not mandatory (Rule 4).
5. Decide whether the brand needs bilingual Chinese/Latin presence, and if so, decide per-brief
   which language leads in size — both size relationships are legitimate outcomes of this system,
   not a single fixed hierarchy (Rule 3).
6. Default to presenting the result as a clean, photoreal rendered mockup with intentional,
   directional studio lighting. Only depart to real environmental photography if the brief
   specifically calls for retail/lifestyle authenticity (Rule 5).
7. If the brand includes more than one SKU or a multi-piece set, compose a family/ensemble shot
   that keeps the repeating mark visible across every piece, rather than generating isolated
   single-item shots with no visual connective tissue (Rule 7).
8. Treat sticker-shaped callout tags, border/frame devices, and exact color/font specification as
   optional or out-of-scope: the first is a legitimate but non-essential flourish (Rule 8); the
   second was never observed and shouldn't be assumed; the third cannot be responsibly specified
   from this evidence base and should be described qualitatively (hue family, contrast behavior)
   rather than invented as an exact value.
