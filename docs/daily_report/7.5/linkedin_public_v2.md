# LinkedIn Post — Visual Search Pilot Study (Public v2)

---

Every "visual search" product is actually serving two different jobs at once: helping people *find* something to look at, and helping people *make* something to use. We ran a small pilot — **58 queries** across **5 platforms** (Google Images, Bing Images, Pinterest, Canva, and Curify) — to see how each one actually shows up on that spectrum, from pure consumption to pure creation.

A few things stood out:

- **Google & Bing anchor the consumption end.** Full coverage across all 58 queries, no login barriers, and the richest taxonomy signal in the pilot — Bing's related-search chips average 39.9 per query, Google's 17.8. If you need a broad recall baseline or a semantic-taxonomy seed, this is where to look.

- **Pinterest sits in the middle, and does it well.** Its label chips are the most granular sub-intent signal we found — not just a broad category, but how people actually refine a search (a "monstera plant care guide" query splits into Indoor, Swiss cheese, Adansonii, Thai constellation). Strongest platform in the set for aesthetic and taste-driven discovery.

- **Canva is the strongest pure creation/template destination** in the pilot — template-first, with high output-type diversity. One caveat worth naming: our review found no stable, query-specific label chips in its current search UI, unlike Google, Bing, and Pinterest, so its intent signal isn't directly comparable on that dimension.

- **Curify sits furthest on the creation end** — template-led, with every result something you can directly generate and adapt rather than just browse. A search-to-creation path that's directly actionable.

We rolled the full comparison into a star-rating scorecard across recall, template orientation, visual diversity, intent-label clarity, and actionability — worth a look if you're thinking about where your own product sits on this spectrum.

Small-scale pilot, June 2026 — directional findings, but the platform differences were already clear.
