### Han Jiang Xue–specific: character/IP and cultural-product language

- **Character/IP language**: zodiac and regional animals (snow leopard, sheep, lion/qilin) treated
  as personified, dignified characters rather than generic mascots — each given individualized
  regalia and a specific narrative framing (concert stage, ceremonial seat). [A], 3 works.
- **Facial/body stylization**: soft, rounded, large-eyed faces; gentle rather than fierce
  expressions, including for guardian-type creatures. [A], 3 works.
- **Traditional Chinese motifs**: dragon, phoenix, cloud, wave, and crane patterns recur as surface
  decoration on both characters and reinterpreted artifacts. [A], 4 works.
- **Decorative density**: high across nearly the entire illustrated sample — ornamentation is a
  default, not an occasional flourish. [B]
- **Narrative composition**: staged scenes (stage, mythological gathering, festival tableau) appear
  more often than isolated character portraits alone. [A], 3 of 10 works staged; 4 more are
  centered single-figure portraits.
- **Pattern/textile translation**: cloisonné and gongbi surface patterns (clouds, waves, cranes)
  read as textile/embroidery-like decoration applied to both figures and reinterpreted objects —
  a consistent "surface pattern as material" habit. [B], hjx_02, 04, 07.
- **Toy/cultural-product translation**: the same personification-in-regalia character language
  (§ above) is applied whether the deliverable is a poster (hjx_01), a toy concept design (hjx_05,
  06), or a museum-grade commemorative object (hjx_07, 08) — the character/motif vocabulary
  transfers across product *types*, which is itself a notable, evidenced capability. [B]

## 10. Design Personality

Reverent toward Chinese cultural heritage without being static or purely decorative — animals and
ancient artifacts are treated as characters with dignity and warmth (soft, rounded, gentle
expressions) rather than as stiff cultural symbols; comfortable moving between award-winning fine
illustration and clean commercial product photography. [B], cross-checked across 10 works.

## 11. Do / Don't

**Do:** default to gold accenting for premium/cultural character or object work; give personified
animals imperial-court-style regalia and gentle, rounded expressions; use ornate framing devices
for hero/portrait pieces; apply cloisonné-style rendering specifically to heritage-artifact
reinterpretation briefs; treat product photography as a legitimate, separate discipline this
illustrator also practices.

**Don't:** assume one fixed illustration technique — this portfolio shows at least 4 distinct
rendering styles; over-claim the stipple technique (§9 #6) or loose ink-wash register (§9 #7) as
stable rules, each rests on a single work; assume the toy/plush concept pieces are photographs of
real manufactured products — they are illustrated concept designs.
